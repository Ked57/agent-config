Why is that the magic number for every developer? It's called the planning fallacy. Human beings are optimistic machines. When you estimate a task, you imagine the path of least resistance. You imagine typing code. You forget about merge conflicts, CI/CD pipeline breaking, the three hours you will spend debugging a library that for some reason hasn't been updated since 2019. Dave from sales slacking you for a quick favor. When a stakeholder asks for a date, they aren't trying to trap you. They are anxious. They have a budget. They have a board meeting. They want certainty. A specific date, March 3rd, feels like control. But a date based on zero research is a hallucination. If you give a single number, a point estimate for a complex task, you have a 90% chance of being wrong. Would you get on a plane that has a 90% chance of crashing? No, I don't think so. So, why do you drive your career that way? Here is a tool that saves you. It's called the cone of uncertainty. This is standard engineering theory. At the beginning of the project where you usually are when they ask for a date, you know the least amount of information you will ever know. At this stage, industry data shows your estimate can be off by 400%. That is not you being bad at your job. That is the nature of software. As you write code, the cone narrows, you learn the truth. But if you gave a hard deadline at the start, you trapped yourself here. You promised precision when all you got was a fog. You need to stop selling certainty and start selling probability. Here is how you do it. Rule number one, never give a single date. If you give a single date, you are 100% wrong. Instead, use a confidence check. When they ask when is it done, you say based on what I know right now, my high confidence estimate is 6 week. If everything goes perfectly, it could be 3 weeks. but I'm planning for six. They might push back. They might say, "I need a specific date for the launch." You stand your ground. You say, "I can say 3 weeks to make you happy right now." Sure, but we will both be screaming at each other in a month when I miss it. I prefer to give you a date I can actually hit. Let's plan for six. Junior promises the best case scenario. Professionals promise the worst case scenario and deliver early. Rule number two, sometimes the cone of uncertainty is too wide. You literally have no idea. In this case, do not guess. Never use what agile methodologies call a spike. A spike is a time boxed investigation. You're buying information. Here is a script. You sound like you care about the outcome, not just getting out of the meeting. You're trading two days of time for 100% more accuracy. That is a good trade.
